# Against: where the combination fails

The evidence that a person plus a model is not always better.

## The meta-analysis

- Vaccaro 2024 pooled 106 experiments and 370 effect sizes. Human-AI combinations did worse than the best of human or AI alone: Hedges' g of -0.23, confidence interval -0.39 to -0.07 (secondary, read in the abstract).
- Decision tasks lost; content-creation tasks gained.
- When the human beat the AI alone, the combination gained. When the AI beat the human alone, it lost.
- The authors name possible publication bias and varied designs.

## The jagged frontier

- Dell'Acqua 2023: outside the model's reach, workers using it were 19% less likely to produce correct solutions (secondary).

## The paradox

- Raisch 2021: augmentation cannot be separated from automation; stressing either one creates reinforcing cycles with bad outcomes.

## What it does to the family

- A rule that the person decides is not a guarantee of a better result.
- A claim of a gain must compare human alone, model alone and the combination.
- The task type matters: the family works on content creation, which is the side that gained.
