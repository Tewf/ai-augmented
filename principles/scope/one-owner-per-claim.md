# One owner per claim

**Claim:** a thing is in the scope of one member of the family, and every other member links it.

- **Grade:** convention, argued from the standard's one-place-per-fact principle; untested for scope tables.
- **Evidence:** the family's rule on the [front page](../../README.md#the-rules); [one place per fact](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics/blob/65e42472e2d4cbfd994c2a86e95a47e5dc7301ca/principles/collection/one-place-per-fact.md) in the standard.
- **Check:** `scope-overlap` (heuristic: two members share a phrase of three words in their in rows) and `scope-overlap-semantic` (heuristic: two in rows of different members reach a cosine of 0.70).
- **Reader styles:** none.

## What it asks

- Each member states what it holds in the in rows of its scope table.
- A thing another member holds is an out row that links that member.
- Two members that claim the same phrase are a finding: one owns it, the other links it.

## Why

- This is [one place per fact](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics/blob/65e42472e2d4cbfd994c2a86e95a47e5dc7301ca/principles/collection/one-place-per-fact.md) applied to scopes: knowledge that must change together lives in one place.
- Two members claiming one thing are two places to change, and they drift.

## Limits

- The check compares words, not meaning. Two rows may say the same thing in other words, or share a phrase and differ.
- On the six members today it finds no shared phrase. That shows the tables can be read, not that they do not overlap in meaning.
- No study compares one repository per problem with fewer, wider ones.
